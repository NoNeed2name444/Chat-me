## Patterns: what the top 5 share

Top 5 by US rating count among med-student study apps: AMBOSS, Lecturio, UWorld, Osmosis, AnkiMobile (#1 paid Education).

1. **Shot 1 is a category promise, not a feature:** a 4–7-word line in the brand colour above or beside a framed device (four of five; Anki shows a raw screen).
2. **One brand colour floods the set**, and the icon is a flat tile with a white glyph and wordmark.
3. **Shot 2 sells breadth, shot 3 sells content quality:** quantities and topic or exam lists, then an explanation, overlay or quiz detail.
4. **Subtitles are keyword strings** of exams and content nouns; three of five use all 30 characters.
5. **One description template:** "trusted by N million" in the first two lines or first bullet, ALL-CAPS headings, long bullets, superlatives, subscription legalese last. Every set sells the vendor's library; the student's own material never appears.

## Gaps: what none do well

1. **Verification and provenance are invisible.** No study app shows where an item came from or how it was checked, and the student's own lectures never appear; only clinician-only OpenEvidence shows an evidence grade.
2. **Speaking and listening are absent.** No spoken examiner or audio mode; OSCE appears only in Geeky Medics, as a text list.
3. **Honest tone is rare and unpolished where present.** Anki is candid but dated and unframed, with the lowest rating (4.0). "Pass", "ace", "score higher" and "#1" are the norm.
4. **Weak synthetic data.** Oncourse repeats one subject label on every ring and shows law content; iatroX's shot 2 is nearly empty; Geeky Medics repeats a caption; captions run 8–10 words.
5. **English-only, US-first.** UKMLA and PLAB appear only in body text; no Arabic, no MENA framing, no dark theme.

## Opportunity for Stethoscore: where we differentiate

Mapped to §23b's first-three rule (promise, how it works, differentiation) and §23c's honest voice:

- **Shot 1, core promise: the student's own lecture becoming practice.** Lecture slide or recording on the left, generated questions on the right. Caption ≤6 words, e.g. "Your lectures, turned into practice." Keep the conventions users already read: framed device, top caption, one brand colour (sky-to-royal blue). No "master", "ace", "pass".
- **Shot 2, how it works: record, upload or snap a slide → ten modes.** Caption "One lecture. Ten study modes." Mode grid from one fictional lecture, realistic synthetic data, no PII.
- **Shot 3, differentiation: the accuracy engine.** An item with confidence, sources and a why-not panel. Caption "Every item checked. Sources shown." Unoccupied space; §23b names this layer the differentiator.
- **Subtitle.** Competitors spend 30 characters on exam acronyms; keywords can go in the 100-character field. Candidates: "Study from your own lectures" (28), "Your lectures, made practice" (28), "Lectures into questions & OSCE" (30). §23c weighs benefit against indexed keyword weight.
- **Shots 4–10:** OSCE spoken examiner, audio and commute mode, Arabic–English toggle, Midnight dark theme, Study Lens. None appears in any competitor's first three. Social proof only if real.
- **Icon.** Competitors are abstract glyphs or wordmarks and blue is the most crowded colour (UWorld, Oncourse, Anatomy Atlas, Medscape, Anki), so the stethoscope silhouette must carry recognition. Test A+ legibility at 60 px; keep "A+" out of captions so it never reads as an outcome promise.
- **Rule check.** Oncourse's Compete and Streaks tiles and "rated 4.8+" laurel, AMBOSS's "score higher" footnote and OpenEvidence's "#1" are what §23c forbids. Case shots show the reasoning step, not a diagnosis-titled result.
